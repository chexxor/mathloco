Q3. Spacing grid - 4px or 8px?

Measured: a 4px grid fits 9 of 9 spacing values already in use; an 8px grid fits 6 of 9 - it would move 4, 12 and 20px. 8px is the more disciplined choice and the more common one; 4px keeps the current density (the tight py-1.5, gap-2, pl-4 of the nav and chips) without changing it.

4px base (recommended)

Half-steps stay legal, so nav and chips keep their current rhythm; the visible sizes above are Fibonacci-ish, which is where your golden-precision instinct has a legitimate home (see Q4's note).

8px base

Fewer decisions, harder to drift. Measured cost: 3 of 9 current values move, which mostly loosens the nav and the small chips.

On the golden ratio specifically - the honest number. Measured: 1.618 fits only 3 of 7 sizes and moves 17px; even 1.5 fits 4/7 and moves 18px. A single-step ratio of 1.618 is the right idea for proportions, not for a seven-step reading ramp. If phi is to be in the system, put it where it pays: the layout split (today lg:col-span-8/4 = exactly 2:1; a grid-cols-13 8/5 split = 1.6), the hero block, and the Fibonacci spacing series in Q3 (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64). That keeps the geometry real instead of decorative - which is the difference between "golden" as a claim and "golden" as a measurement.

Q6. Default easing

Measured: 18 of 36 transition declarations name no duration (Tailwind's 150ms default), 18 name 300ms, and zero name an easing (so ease is doing the work). Cloudflare's own docs recommend "ease-out, or linear for color" for motion that feels responsive.

ease-out for movement, linear for colour (recommended)

Objects arrive and stop; colour does not overshoot. Keeps the 300ms already in use for hero and section transitions, and gives the 18 nameless transitions an actual default instead of 150ms-by-accident.

keep ease-in-out

What 12 of the transitions effectively already do. Slower to settle; fine, but it makes the site feel a beat behind the pointer.
