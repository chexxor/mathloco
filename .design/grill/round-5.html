The nav column is the same finding as round 4's defect, and it is not a type decision. The row has 343px of content width at a 375px viewport (16px side padding) and every face wants 503–567px on one line: the narrowest (PT Sans) is 160px too wide, the widest 224px. No face choice saves the nav — the row must wrap, or the label set must shrink, as a component decision next round. So choose the type answer on legibility and character, not on whether it fits the nav.

Q2 · which spacing steps (this one is almost answered by the code itself)

S3 = 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 fits 65 of 66 writes (98.5%); the only orphan is mt-5 (20px, 1 write). S2 drops 64 and moves 3 writes; S1 drops 4 and 64 and moves 5. Q3 · how many font sizes, and what line height means

46 font-size writes across 9 rendered sizes (14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48, 60 px). The 1.200 ratio is ratified; what is open is how many steps, and that changes how big the hero title is allowed to be. “Travel” is the total distance every current size has to move, weighted by how often it is used.

T1 · six steps — travel 70.3px

A proof, four ways.

13.3 16 19.2 23 33.2 47.8 — the hero's 60px folds to 47.8 (−12.2). Tightest discipline, biggest rewrite.

T2 · eight steps — travel 66.7px

A proof, four ways.

13.3 16 19.2 23 27.7 33.2 39.8 47.8 — same top step, but 27.7 and 39.8 exist so headings stop jumping 3.2px.

T3 · nine steps (+57.3) — travel 47.7px

A proof, four ways.

…39.8 47.8 57.3 — keeps today's hero size (−2.7) and gives the numerals a display step, which is what the timeline and the interview series asked for.

Line height: 58 of 71 writes (81.7%) already inherit it from the size token, so that part is settled by rule — leading travels with size, and the one-off leading-6/9/10 values get deleted. What is open is the two ends of the ramp, and both are measurable:

Tailwind's own pairing — body 1.50 (24px at 16px) · top of ramp 0.98

And a floor for display type. Tailwind pairs 47.8px with 1.00 and 57.3px with 0.98. I measured PT Serif's own ink box instead of assuming one: at 60px weight 700, “Typographic” inks 61.3px deep — 47.3px above the baseline and 14.0px below. At leading 0.98 two lines of that heading collide by 2.5px, at 1.00 by 1.3px, and clear by 1.7px at 1.05. So the rule I propose is: leading comes from the size token, but display sizes floor at 1.05 and serif body copy reads at 1.6.

what I would have done that you did not ask for

I nearly asked “which sans?” without measuring what the site currently renders. It renders nothing we chose: --font-sans names “Inter var”, which no file provides, so chrome is the visitor's OS face. And I nearly quoted the 107,680 B font figure as a download. Measured, a visitor gets 33,116 B. Those two corrections change the arithmetic of the ruling: option B costs a visitor +56,420 B over today (the real bold serif + one variable sans), not “free because we delete cyrillic”.

What is still owed after this round: components (control heights from the spacing steps, button variants, the density of a /schedule row) and then the pattern set (zero-upcoming empty state, stale snapshot, Discord error+retry, cancelled event, and “time not set” — two of your four real November dates carry no clock).
