Round 21 — the logo in Byrne's 1847 manner, in this palette

If you can, also create it in the style of John Byrne’s 1847 Euclid illustrations, where it makes heavy use of primary colors. If you can do that, use our website’s color palette, though.

The source, retrieved rather than remembered: Oliver Byrne, "The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid, in Which Coloured Diagrams and Symbols Are Used Instead of Letters for the Greater Ease of Learners", London 1847 (printed by Charles Whittingham, Chiswick Press). Three of its facts drive the rows below — colour replaces letters (the title says so: a line is named by its colour, and the prose refers to "the squares of the blue and red lines"); Byrne 1847 insists the colour is not ornament ("we do not introduce colours for the purpose of entertainment… but to assist the mind"); and the ink is red, yellow, blue and black on white paper.

The tension this round prices rather than hides: Byrne colours a line to name it. This logo's claim is that its two lines are the same kind of line — parallel where they leave the equator, and crossing only because the surface is curved. Colouring them differently is the most literal Byrne reading and the one that costs the logo its claim. Row C is on the table to be refused.

Nothing here is applied. Colour on this site is operator-ratified (D316), so a Byrne colouring arrives as a priced specimen and the ruling stays the operator's. Every photograph is the built page's own bytes with the eight shape colours rewritten to var(--color-…) in a copy of dist/client, measured by the same two probes check:logo runs (logo-geometry.mjs, logo-pixels.mjs) at the three widths that bracket the logo's box, plus the share card's own document on the ink ground. Row C is the colouring the site ships, and it passes every check — it is found by matching every row against LOGO_COLOURS in src/lib/logo.mjs, not by naming a letter here — and that is what makes the other rows' numbers mean something: if the shipped colouring had failed, the mutation would be broken and no row here would be trustworthy.
