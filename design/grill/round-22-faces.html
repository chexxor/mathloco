Round 22 — Byrne faces for the club mark: priced, not applied

the second half of the seventh ruling on this mark (D483): "but also show me options for coloring faces of the shape also in the Byrne style"

The source, retrieved rather than remembered: Oliver Byrne, "The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid, in Which Coloured Diagrams and Symbols Are Used Instead of Letters for the Greater Ease of Learners", London 1847. What its own words license here is the second half of the ruling: Byrne names regions by colour exactly as he names lines — the prose speaks of "the squares of the blue and red lines" — and his fills are flat, which is why they print cheaply and why a palette tint is the wrong tool for one.

Nothing here is applied. Colour on this site is operator-ratified (D316), so a face colouring arrives as a priced specimen and the pick stays the operator's. Every photograph is the built page's own bytes with the eight shape colours rewritten to var(--color-…) and the filled region added, in a copy of dist/client, measured by the same two probes check:mark runs (design/mark-geometry.mjs, design/mark-pixels.mjs) at 320 / 640 / 1240 px plus the share card's own document at 1200×630. Row C0 is the colouring the site ships, with nothing filled, and it passes every check — found by matching the rows' line maps against MARK_COLOURS in src/lib/brand-mark.mjs, not by naming a letter here — so every other number below is a delta from a measured line.

1. Area: the faces this drawing actually bounds, measured twice

A face has an area, and it is measured two ways: projected (dense sampling and a shoelace over a boundary built from the mark's own curves) and on the sphere this drawing implies (Girard's excess for the triangle between the meridians, $2\cdot\text{angle}\cdot r^2$ for the lunes above and below the near equator arc). The two agree to under 2%, and the round refuses if they do not: a region named by a colour should at least be named by a geometry. 2. Which lines sit on which face (sampled, not asserted)

A fill changes the bar a line is held to only where the line actually lies on the fill. 4. The two-bar window: what a legal face costs per hue family (token hues, and the gold @theme does not declare)

For every hue family the rows use, at that family's own chroma, the round solves the lightness window in which a fill (a) clears 1.35 against its ground and (b) leaves every line drawn over it at 3 against the fill. The upper edge is set by the ground, the lower edge by the lightest line token over the face. For a family the palette already declares the round paints the mid of that window, the point furthest from both bars. Gold is the exception and the reason matters: gold-ness is not a contrast ratio, it is how a hue ~75 reads, and round 21's floor for that (L 60) is a stated judgement, not a ratified bar — so a gold face takes max(window low end, L 60), the deepest lightness that still reads as gold, and when the window never reaches 60 it takes the palest end it can and says so ("BELOW the gold floor"). Each row's own line under the swatch names which end was taken and why.

8. How to read this page, and what the delegate recommends

  • Row C0 is the shipped colouring with nothing filled — the control, and the only row the site's own gates can measure today without re-ruling them.
  • Rows F1–F5 fill one or two of the faces the drawing really bounds, in a hue the palette already declares. F1 fills the spherical triangle between the two meridians (25.50% of the visible face); F2 the south lune (31.57%); F3 the north lune (68.45%); F5 the near hemisphere, which is the disc (100%); F4 two disjoint regions, which is the literal Byrne sentence — two colours so the prose can point at either without a letter.
  • Rows F6, F7, F8, F9 fill the same faces in the hue the operator asked for and @theme does not declare, and the only difference between them is area: F6 the spherical triangle (25.50%), F9 the north lune (68.45%), F7 the near hemisphere, which is the disc (100.02% — the most gold the drawing can carry, with 8 of the 8 painted shapes lying over it on paper), F8 two disjoint regions, gold plus the club red, which is Byrne's two-colour sentence with the yellow present.
  • Why gold is harder here than on a tab. A token hue may sit anywhere in its window; a gold has to sit at or above the lightness where hue 75 still reads as gold (L 60, a stated judgement, not a bar). On this mark that constraint inverts which ground has room: on the hero the two-bar window only reaches gold at its palest end, and that end is within 0.05 delta-OKLab of a brand colour; on the card no lightness in the window reaches the floor at all while keeping the paper-coloured lines legible. §4b prints both as numbers.
  • The squeeze, stated plainly. A face has to be far enough from the ground to read as a region and far enough from the lightest line drawn on it to keep that line legible. The windows in §4 are only a few tenths of a lightness wide, and their midpoints sit at 1.63 at their most far from the ground. A legal Byrne wash here is a quiet region, not Byrne's saturated block: Byrne's flatness is what made his fills cheap to print, and this palette's flatness is what keeps a chip ground from reading as raised. Both are flat, for opposite reasons.
  • What each row costs in tokens: every row re-uses row C's eight line colours unchanged, and adds 1–2 new hero token(s) and 1–2 on the card, unless the solve lands within 0.05 delta-OKLab of a token the palette already has, in which case the row re-uses it — printed per fill above.
