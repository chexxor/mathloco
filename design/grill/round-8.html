Q4. The measure at wide widths — a cap, or leave the lines long

Measured on the shipped page: the two section intro paragraphs (text-lede) run 69 and 76 characters across a 1164px box at 1240 — over the reading band even today. Bigger type moves them further over unless the box gets a cap. A cap of about 70 characters is ~660px at the candidate lede size, which is what the hero column already is (672px). The narrow columns need nothing: at 320 the body box is 228px and the line is 28 characters, which is short, not long.

What the measurements say before any option

  1. Body copy on this site runs 29-38 characters per line at every shipped width (measured: median 30.1 ch at 1240, 29.0 at 390, ~24 at 320; node design/line-length.mjs). That is under the 40-75 ch reading band, so nothing here blocks a size increase on line-length grounds — the site's lines are already short.
  2. The exception is at 1240: two lede paragraphs run 69-76 ch across a 1164px box. Bigger type there runs further past the band unless the prose box gets a cap.
  3. "The serif reads smaller than the chrome" is not an x-height story on this pair: PT Serif x-height 0.50 em, Public Sans 0.52 em (4% apart, design/fonts/metrics.json). The serif is narrower (0.533 vs 0.612 em per average char), so it buys longer lines at the same size — a bump to the content step costs fewer characters than the same bump to a chrome step.
  4. Control heights are derived, not chosen: height = line box + 2 x pad, so a label change moves 48/40. Each option's control heights are printed under its buttons, and the specimen measures what actually landed.
  5. The display step has a measured cap rule. Where a candidate's display step cannot fit its longest word in the 276px phone box, the cap column flags it.
